Fraction ↔ Percentage ▾

Memorize these cold — converting a fraction to % (or back) in your head saves 15-20 seconds per question.

Multiplication Shortcuts ▾

× 5: multiply by 10, then halve. 84×5 → 840÷2 = 420.
× 9: multiply by 10, subtract the number. 47×9 → 470−47 = 423.
× 11 (2-digit): split the digits, put their sum in the middle. 45×11 → 4_(4+5)_5 = 495. If sum > 9, carry the 1 left.
× 25: multiply by 100, divide by 4. 68×25 → 6800÷4 = 1700.
Squaring numbers ending in 5: n(n+1) then append 25. 65² → 6×7=42 → 4225.
Squaring near 50: (25 + last two digits' offset) then square the offset. For 47² → 50−47=3 → 25−3=22, then 03²=09 → 2209.
Squaring near 100: 96² → offset=−4 → 96−4=92, then (−4)²=16 → 9216. Works for 90-110 range.
Base method (near 100) multiplication: 97×94 → offsets −3,−6 → cross-subtract: 97−6=91 (or 94−3=91), then (−3)×(−6)=18 → 9118.

Percentage Speed Tricks ▾

10% of a number: shift decimal one place left. Build everything else off this: 5%=half of 10%, 20%=double 10%, 15%=10%+5%, 1%=10%÷10.
x% of y = y% of x — always pick whichever is easier to compute. 16% of 25 = 25% of 16 = 4.
Successive % change shortcut: combine two changes a%,b% as a+b+(ab/100). Never average them.
+x% then −x% (or vice versa) always nets a decrease of x²/100 percent.

Simplification / BODMAS Speed ▾

Solve innermost brackets first, then "of", then ÷ and × left-to-right, then + and − left-to-right. Don't skip steps mentally past 2 operations — that's where silly mistakes cost time on recheck.
Scan the full expression once before writing anything — look for numbers that cancel out or combine easily (e.g. two terms that sum to a round number) and do those first.
Convert ugly fractions to decimals only if the decimal terminates quickly (halves, quarters, fifths); otherwise keep as fractions — decimals with repeating digits waste time.

Reasoning Speed Tips ▾

Blood relations: draw the family tree as you read, one symbol per relation (+/− for gender, arrows for generation). Never try to hold it in your head past 2 relations.
Coding-decoding: check letter-position shift first (A=1...Z=26) before assuming a complex pattern — most codes are ±constant shifts or simple reversals.
Direction sense: always sketch a quick compass cross and plot each move as you read the sentence, don't try to track direction mentally.
Series/analogy: check difference-of-differences (2nd order) if 1st order differences aren't constant, before assuming a multiplicative pattern.

Exam-Day Speed Strategy ▾

Skip-and-return: if a question takes more than ~40-45 seconds without a clear path, mark and move on. Returning fresh after finishing easier ones is faster than getting stuck.
Section order: do your strongest section first to bank easy marks and build momentum, then attempt your weakest section with remaining time.
Elimination over calculation: in MCQs, check if 2 options can be eliminated by a rough estimate (units digit, rounding, sign) before doing the full calculation.
Don't verify what you're sure of: re-checking correct answers burns time that's better spent on unattempted questions — trust a confident first pass.
