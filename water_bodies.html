National River Linking Project (NRLP)

Overview

NRLP proposes linking 37 Himalayan and Peninsular rivers via a network of 30 links and 3,000 storage reservoirs. Conceptualized by K.L. Rao and later by Arthur Cotton. Revived post-2002 by NDA government.

River Pollution & CPCB Classification

CPCB Priority Basins

CPCB identifies priority rivers by BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand) load. Rivers are categorised into Priority-I, II, III based on pollution severity.

Most Polluted Rivers (CPCB)

  • Ganga — 150+ drains; industrial effluents from tanneries (Kanpur), textile units
  • Yamuna — 80% of Delhi's sewage; Wazirpur, Okhla industrial zones
  • Damodar — "Sorrow of Bengal"; heavy metal pollution; Jharkhand/WB
  • Subernarekha — Jharkhand; uranium tailings; heavy metals
  • Mithi — Mumbai; treated as open drain
  • Cooum & Adyar — Chennai; severely polluted
  • Sabarmati — Ahmedabad; textile effluents

BOD Classification (CPCB)

  • Class A (BOD ≤1 mg/L) — Drinking water without conventional treatment
  • Class B (BOD ≤3 mg/L) — Outdoor bathing
  • Class C (BOD ≤3 mg/L) — Drinking water with conventional treatment
  • Class D (BOD ≤6 mg/L) — Wildlife, fisheries
  • Class E (BOD ≤6 mg/L) — Irrigation, industrial cooling
Key fact: Ganga near Varanasi often has BOD >6 mg/L — Class E or worse.

Endorheic (Internal Drainage) Rivers & Basins

What is Endorheic Drainage?

A closed drainage basin with no outlet to the ocean. Water collects in lakes (often saline), sinks into the ground, or evaporates. Covers ~18% of Earth's land surface.

Major Endorheic Basins

  • Caspian Sea basin — Volga, Ural rivers drain here; world's largest endorheic basin
  • Aral Sea basin — Central Asia; one of the worst ecological disasters (now nearly dry)
  • Tarim Basin — Xinjiang, China; Tarim River drains into Lop Nur
  • Great Basin — Nevada, USA; Colorado Plateau rim
  • Lake Chad basin — Shrinking rapidly (90% reduction since 1960s)
  • Sambhar Lake basin — Rajasthan, India (endorheic saline lake)
Aral Sea Disaster: Soviet irrigation diverted Amu Darya and Syr Darya for cotton cultivation. The Aral Sea shrank by 90% between 1960–2009. Salinization, dust storms, stranded ships — classic case of human-induced ecological collapse. Frequently asked in UPSC environment questions.
UN Watercourses Convention (1997): Governs non-navigational uses of international watercourses. Principles: equitable & reasonable utilisation, no significant harm, notification obligation. India has NOT ratified.

Chilika Lake — Deep Dive

Chilika Lake
Odisha · Puri, Khurda, Ganjam districts

Asia's largest brackish water lagoon (1,100 km²). Separated from Bay of Bengal by a narrow sandbar — opens seasonally. Declared Ramsar site in 1981 (India's first Ramsar site, along with Keoladeo Ghana). Chilika Development Authority (CDA) manages it.

Migratory birds — 160+ species Irrawaddy Dolphin Flamingos Jackals Olive Ridley turtles
Ecological Threats to Chilika
  • Siltation reducing depth and area
  • Mouth closure → reduced salinity → change in ecology
  • Invasive water hyacinth (Eichhornia crassipes)
  • Prawn farming (aquaculture) in sensitive zones
  • Over-fishing depleting fish stocks
  • Agricultural runoff; fertilizer & pesticide pollution
  • Removal from Montreux Record (2002) after restoration

Loktak Lake — Deep Dive

Loktak Lake
Manipur · 4 districts

Largest freshwater lake in Northeast India. Famous for phumdis — floating masses of soil, vegetation, and organic matter unique to Loktak. Home to Keibul Lamjao National Park — world's only floating national park. Sanctuary for Sangai (Brow-antlered Deer) — dancing deer.

Ramsar site Floating NP Sangai deer CR
Threats to Loktak
  • Ithai Barrage (1983) — altered hydrology; phumdis fragmented
  • Siltation from deforestation in catchment
  • Encroachment by villages on phumdis
  • Pollution from Imphal city and agricultural runoff
  • Decline of Sangai habitat
  • Added to Montreux Record (1993)

Lonar Lake — Global Uniqueness

Lonar Crater Lake
Buldana, Maharashtra · ~52,000 years old

Formed by a meteorite impact on the Deccan Trap basaltic rock — making it the only hypervelocity meteorite crater lake in basaltic rock in the world. The lake is alkaline and saline (pH ~10.5). Contains unique halophilic (salt-loving) bacteria and archaea. Declared Ramsar site in 2020. Has both a crater lake (saline) and an outer fresh water lake. Surrounded by Lonar Crater Wildlife Sanctuary.

Hypervelocity impact Basaltic rock pH ~10.5 Ramsar 2020 Unique microbial life
UPSC One-liner: Lonar is the only saline volcanic crater lake formed in basaltic rock in the world (technically meteor impact, not volcanic — but the surrounding rock is basaltic Deccan Trap). This distinction appears in exam questions.

Lakes Under Threat

Dal Lake, Kashmir

Shrunk from 75 km² to 18 km²; encroachment by over 100,000 households; floating gardens (Radhs) reduce water body; eutrophication from nutrients; National Mission for Clean Lakes (NMCL) program ongoing.

Bellandur Lake, Bengaluru

Urban lake — severely eutrophied; froth and fire events (2015, 2017) — phosphate-laden froth caught fire; National Green Tribunal (NGT) ordered restoration; encroachment by IT corridors.

Deepor Beel, Assam

Ramsar site near Guwahati; threatened by railway line cutting through it, municipal garbage dumping, and urban expansion. Significant for migratory birds and Asian Elephant habitat.

Coral Reefs — India & World

Coral Reefs: Occupy less than 0.1% of ocean area but support ~25% of all marine species. Built by coral polyps depositing calcium carbonate skeletons. Require: clear, warm (23–29°C), shallow, saltwater. Three types: Fringing reefs, Barrier reefs, Atolls.
India's Coral Reefs
  • Lakshadweep — Best-developed coral atolls in India; 36 atolls; coral bleaching threat
  • Andaman & Nicobar — Fringing reefs; high biodiversity; tsunami impacted some reefs in 2004
  • Gulf of Mannar — 21 small islands; fringing reefs; Biosphere Reserve; dugong habitat
  • Gulf of Kutch — Only coral reefs on mainland India; Marine NP; bleaching events 2015–16
World's Notable Coral Reefs
  • Great Barrier Reef, Australia — World's largest coral system (2,300km); UNESCO WHS; mass bleaching 2016, 2017, 2020, 2022
  • Mesoamerican Reef — Caribbean; 2nd largest barrier reef; Mexico to Honduras
  • New Caledonia — Pacific; UNESCO WHS; 2nd most biodiverse reef
  • Red Sea Coral Reef — More heat-tolerant; survives at 32°C
  • Coral Triangle — Philippines, Indonesia, Malaysia, PNG — "Amazon of the Sea"

Ocean Acidification

Cause

Oceans absorb ~30% of anthropogenic CO₂. CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃ (carbonic acid) → H⁺ + HCO₃⁻. Increased H⁺ lowers ocean pH. Ocean pH has dropped from 8.2 to 8.1 since industrialisation (~26% increase in acidity, logarithmic scale).

Effects & UPSC Angle
  • Dissolves calcium carbonate shells of corals, molluscs, pteropods
  • Coral bleaching + dissolution = reef collapse
  • Disrupts fish behaviour (altered CO₂ sensing)
  • Affects plankton — base of marine food chain
  • Arctic/Southern Oceans most vulnerable (cold water absorbs more CO₂)
  • UPSC: Linked to UNFCCC, IPCC reports, Paris Agreement targets

Glacial Retreat — Data & Impact

Key Data Points
  • Hindu Kush Himalaya hosts 56,000+ glaciers — "Third Pole" (largest freshwater reserve outside polar regions)
  • HKH glaciers lost ~40% volume since Little Ice Age; accelerating since 1970s
  • Gangotri retreating at 22m/year; net retreat since 1780 = ~2,850m
  • IPCC AR6 (2021): Even at 1.5°C warming, HKH glaciers lose 36% by 2100; at 2°C = 49%; at 4°C = 75%
  • Siachen: losing ice but also heavily polluted by military waste
  • India's glaciers feed Indus, Ganga, Brahmaputra — water for 500+ million people
Consequences for India
  • Short-term gain: Initially increased meltwater (glacial surge) → floods
  • Long-term deficit: Reduced perennial flow → water insecurity
  • Hydropower disruption (Himachal, Uttarakhand, J&K)
  • Agricultural collapse in irrigation-dependent Indo-Gangetic Plain
  • Increased GLOF frequency and flood risk
  • Drinking water scarcity for mountain communities
  • Pakistan more vulnerable — 75% of Indus flow from glaciers

Glacial Lake Outburst Floods (GLOFs)

Mechanism of GLOF

Glacial lakes form when meltwater is dammed by moraine, ice, or bedrock. As the lake grows and the dam weakens (from melting, erosion, or seismic activity), it fails catastrophically — releasing millions of cubic metres of water, ice, rocks and sediment into river valleys.

Chamoli Disaster (February 7, 2021)
Uttarakhand · Rishiganga & Dhauliganga valleys

A rockfall + glacial lake outburst triggered a catastrophic flash flood in Chamoli district. Destroyed Rishiganga Power Project and NTPC Tapovan-Vishnugad project. Over 200 people dead/missing. Raised questions about development in ecologically sensitive Himalayan river valleys and the Char Dham project.

Anthropogenic + naturalTapovan project
Vulnerable Regions in India
  • Uttarakhand — Alaknanda, Bhagirathi, Dhauliganga valleys; multiple hydro projects
  • Himachal Pradesh — Beas, Chenab catchments; Kullu-Manali flash floods
  • Sikkim — South Lhonak Lake GLOF (October 2023) — destroyed Teesta Stage III dam; major flooding
  • Ladakh — Shyok River; Siachen area
  • Arunachal Pradesh — Subansiri basin; Himalayan seismic zone

Cryosphere & Climate Regulation

What is the Cryosphere?

The portion of Earth's surface where water exists in solid form: ice sheets, glaciers, sea ice, permafrost, snow cover, ice caps, frozen ground. Covers ~15% of Earth's land surface and much of the polar oceans seasonally.

Climate Regulation Roles

  • Albedo effect: Ice reflects 80–90% solar radiation; loss → warming acceleration (positive feedback)
  • Freshwater storage: 70% of world's freshwater stored in glaciers/ice sheets
  • Ocean circulation: Meltwater → salinity changes → AMOC disruption
  • Sea level regulation: Thermal expansion + ice melt = SLR
  • Permafrost carbon: Permafrost holds ~1.5 trillion tonnes of carbon; thawing releases methane & CO₂
  • Arctic amplification: Arctic warming 2–4× faster than global average
IPCC SROCC (2019): Special Report on Ocean and Cryosphere in a Changing Climate — projected 0.61–1.10m global mean SLR by 2100 under high emissions. Ice-sheet instability could push this higher.
Arctic Sea Ice: Summer minimum Arctic sea ice extent declined by ~13%/decade since 1979. "Ice-free Arctic summers" possible by 2030s under current trajectories — opens Northern Sea Route, geopolitical contest for Arctic resources.

Namami Gange Programme
Launched: 2015 · Ministry: Jal Shakti · Budget: ₹20,000 crore (Phase 1)

Integrated conservation mission to prevent pollution and rejuvenate the Ganga. Works under National Mission for Clean Ganga (NMCG) since 2011. Key objectives: Nirmal Ganga (clean Ganga) & Aviral Ganga (continuous flow).

Key Components

  • Sewage Treatment Plants (STPs) in 118 Urban Local Bodies
  • Industrial Effluent Treatment along Ganga banks
  • River Surface Cleaning — ghats & floodplains
  • Afforestation on floodplains (Harihar Van)
  • Biological Conservation: Biodiversity Conservation
  • Public Awareness: Ganga Gram Yojana
  • E-flows: Minimum environmental flows mandated (2018 Notification)

Achievements (Key Claims)

  • Dissolved oxygen improved at several stretches
  • Dolphin population surveys show modest recovery
  • 4,000+ km of riverbanks cleaned
  • 97 ghats & 11 crematoria renovated
  • Arthganga — economic activities linked to Ganga conservation
  • 5 River Cities Alliance (Varanasi, Kanpur, Rishikesh…)
  • World Bank — $400 million loan support
Criticism: CAG and CPCB reports note persistent water quality issues; BOD still above Class B in many stretches; pace of STP construction slow; industrial violations continue in Kanpur tanneries.

National Water Policy 2012
Ministry: Jal Shakti (then Water Resources) · Under review for new policy

Key Provisions

  • Water as common pool resource; no individual property rights
  • Priority: Drinking water > Agriculture > Industry > Ecology > Non-consumptive
  • Basin/sub-basin as unit for planning
  • Groundwater regulation under law
  • Water pricing for equitable allocation
  • River Basin Authorities recommended

Concerns & Gaps

  • Water is a State subject (7th Schedule) — Centre has limited direct authority
  • Climate change not adequately addressed
  • No universal right to water guaranteed
  • New National Water Policy under draft as of 2024
